AOI Top-and-Bottom Inspection Line

Context

The original AOI configuration inspected one surface at a time. Parts needed additional handling between passes, which increased takt time and introduced opportunities for orientation mistakes and duplicated handling.

The project redesigned the inspection flow so the top and bottom surfaces could be scanned in the same production cycle, while integrating third-party loading and unloading equipment.

The problem

The challenge was not only adding another camera. The complete line needed coordinated motion, safe handoff, stable part presentation, and a result model that could associate both surfaces with the same part identity.

The design goals were:

  • reduce manual handling between top and bottom inspection;
  • preserve part identity across both inspection paths;
  • coordinate AOI, loader, and unloader states;
  • make faults recoverable for operators;
  • keep third-party interfaces isolated from inspection logic.

Solution

The line controller coordinates the loader, inspection stations, transfer mechanism, and unloader through explicit states. Each transition has a precondition, completion signal, timeout, and recovery path.

A shared part identity is created at entry and carried through both scan results. The inspection layer does not need to know the vendor-specific protocol of the loader or unloader; adapters translate equipment signals into the line controller's internal commands and events.

The operator screen exposes the current station, part identity, equipment state, and recovery instruction. This is more useful on a production floor than showing a raw collection of vendor alarms.

Engineering challenges

Timing and synchronization

Top and bottom scans have different acquisition and completion times. The controller waits for both result channels before closing the part cycle, while monitoring timeouts and incomplete data separately.

Third-party integration

Vendor communication is isolated behind an adapter boundary. Handshake signals, busy states, alarm codes, and reset operations are normalized so the rest of the application remains stable if equipment details change.

Recovery

A faulted part must not be silently released. The system moves the line into a known state, records the reason, and guides the operator through retry, manual removal, or quarantine.

Outcome

The generalized redesign supports simultaneous two-sided inspection, reduces intermediate handling, and gives operators a clearer recovery model. The implementation details and performance figures are intentionally generalized for confidentiality.
